Philosophy

Writing is a form of listening
done well before the first word.

"In a digital industry crowded with noise, the winner isn't who posts the most — it's who earns the most trust."

I write copy that treats readers as intelligent human beings, not conversion targets. Every piece starts with one question: who is this really for? Then I build outward — from empathy to argument, from culture to craft.

My background in French Language Education at UNY trained me to think in registers: how meaning shifts across contexts, how tone signals intent, how a single word choice can open or shut a door. That's not a language skill — it's a copywriting superpower.

02
Opinion & Cultural Essay · Education

The Library Nobody Opens: When Universities Silence Their Own Generation

"Student academic work is objectified cultural capital — high-value intellectual output left to decay not because of limited ideas, but limited systems."

This essay argues one of the most uncomfortable truths in Indonesian higher education: every semester, tens of millions of documents — essays on poverty, poems about inequality, analyses of vanishing local cultures — are created and destroyed inside a bureaucratic loop that never touches public life.

The argument hinges on a powerful paradox: Generation Z is the most digitally fluent generation in history, yet the deeper they go into higher education, the more thoroughly the system seals them into a soundproof room. Closes with a concrete proposal rooted in international renewable assignment models, asking campuses to choose: prison or stage?

Method

Three moves.
Every single time.

I
Listen Before Writing
I don't start with sentences. I start with research — the audience's fears, their vocabulary, their trust triggers. Copy written without this is just noise.
II
Anchor in Specifics
Vague claims die. Specific details stick. "86% of Gen Z relies on TikTok for beauty info" is more persuasive than "young people love social media." I work in specifics.
III
Write for the Human
Algorithms change. Human psychology doesn't. I write for what makes people lean forward, not what makes dashboards tick — and the best copy does both.
